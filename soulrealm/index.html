soulrealm · runtime · coming soon

Soulstream is the record. Soulrealm is the room.

A script sitting in a folder isn't doing anything. For it to matter, someone has to run it: give it a machine, an identity, permission to act, and a way to be watched and shut down when it's done. A record of work is not the same as a place to do the work.

Soulstream keeps the record. It gives a realm its topics, its operations, its baselines, its personas, and every trace of who did what. Then it stops, on purpose, at the edge of execution. Soulrealm is the room on the other side of that edge, where the agents and tools of a realm actually run.

Soulrealm launches, supervises, watches, and retires the workloads of a realm, on a laptop to start and across a fleet later. While a workload runs, everything worth keeping flows back into soulstream topics as operations. The scratch state a crashed workload loses was never the point; the history stays in the record, where it always was.

The bet underneath it is soulstream's bet, carried one step further. A running agent is a persona, not a service tier. It holds the same kind of credential as any human in the realm, publishes the same operation record, and is addressed the same way. There is no bot API. Starting it, the work it claims, the moment it finishes: these are ordinary operations on the same log a human reads, not events on a separate dashboard only operators can see.

One control plane

A workload's whole visible life is operations on topics. There is no second coordination system running beside the log. Each workload gets its own freshly minted NATS identity, scoped to just the subjects its persona is allowed to touch, so what a workload can do on the wire is exactly what its persona could do by hand.

What it refuses to become

Soulrealm never becomes the authoritative home of an artefact or its history. That lives in soulstream, and the boundary does not bend. It grants machine personas no elevated standing; agents and tools are peers on the wire, not a privileged tier. And it is not a new orchestrator competing with Kubernetes. If a deployment already runs one, soulrealm runs on top of it rather than in place of it, and the isolation backend, whether a bare process, a container, or a microVM, is a deployment choice rather than a rewrite.

What's true today

Design settled; first milestones landing. The hard early question, what runtime to build on, is decided: soulrealm builds its own runtime, shaped by a close read of NEX but depending on none of it, with the soulstream op-log as its single control plane. Against that design, the first slices now run. An agent launches, posts a turn attributed to its own persona, and has its lifecycle recorded as work operations on the topic. A separate tool workload starts, is found by name, and answers a call from that agent over the realm's own transport, both under the one-identity model.

Not here yet. There is no release to install. Next is a second isolation backend, proving the very same agent and tool declarations run unchanged when the thing underneath them changes. The physical sandbox, fleets across many nodes, and a wider ecosystem of tools are named and deliberately unbuilt, each waiting behind the milestone before it. You can read the design and the honest build log today; there is nothing to go install yet.
